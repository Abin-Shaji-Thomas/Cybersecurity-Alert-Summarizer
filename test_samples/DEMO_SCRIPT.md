# Viva demo script (about 6–7 minutes)

**Before the viva:** start the backend and frontend (see the main README), open http://localhost:5173, and delete old
analyses on the Dashboard so the history is clean. Keep `test_samples/` open in Explorer for drag & drop.
Each upload takes about 8–16 seconds.

| # | Time | Do this | Say this | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00–0:30 | Show the Dashboard and the About page's architecture boxes. | "Analysts get thousands of raw IDS alerts. The app turns them into a few incidents with a plain-English summary. React talks to FastAPI, which runs an NLP pipeline built from the five syllabus units and stores the results in SQLite." | all |
| 2 | 0:30–1:00 | Drag **`bad_format.txt`** onto the upload box, then **`bad_empty.json`**. | "Wrong files are handled cleanly: the .txt is rejected straight away, and the empty JSON is accepted but marked *failed* with a clear reason in the history." | – |
| 3 | 1:00–2:30 | Upload **`04_multi_stage_attack.json`** and click **Open analysis →**. Show the Incidents cards, then the **Timeline** tab. | "These are real alerts from the AIT-ADS dataset. Each alert is first rewritten as an English sentence. TF-IDF vectors and KMeans group them into 14 incidents; the top one is Critical: 172.24.249.224 flooding the web server. On the timeline the colours are attack phases from the dataset's labels: scans, wpscan, dirb, cracking, then privilege escalation, in time order. That is temporal analysis." | IV, V |
| 4 | 2:30–3:30 | Click the Critical card. Point at the template summary, the **repeated / attempted** badges and the slots table. Click **Generate AI summary**, then **Download PDF**. | "The main summary is template filling: slots for what, who, where, when, severity, MITRE and action. The aspect badges say it was repeated and only attempted. The extractive and AI summaries are the comparison; the AI one is fluent but can copy or garble details, so the template is the reliable one." | V |
| 5 | 3:30–4:00 | Go back, open the last incident *Successful sudo to ROOT executed* (or *User successfully changed UID*). | "This one is marked **succeeded**: gmarsh became root. The outcome rule only counts a success that belongs to the attack: from the attacker's IP, or a local event like sudo on a host the attacker hit." | V |
| 6 | 4:00–4:45 | Upload **`synthetic_07_port_scan_many_hosts.json`**, open it, **Entities & Relations** tab. | "Security NER finds IPs, hosts and the Nmap tool. Rules over the entities and the dependency triples give relations: one IP ATTACKED 8 hosts and SCANNED 15 ports, which is the graph." | II, IV |
| 7 | 4:45–5:30 | Upload **`synthetic_06_bruteforce_then_success.json`**, open the **Ask** tab, ask *Did the attacker log in successfully?* and *How many failed logins were there?* | "The chatbot detects the question's intent with rules, answers from the extracted facts and shows supporting alerts found by TF-IDF retrieval. Yes: after 30 failures, 198.51.100.23 logged in as jhall." | V |
| 8 | 5:30–6:30 | Open **NLP Lab**, paste `At 13:20:00, host webserver reported 'Suricata: Alert - ET EXPLOIT Apache log4j RCE Attempt (http ldap) (CVE-2021-44228)' from 203.0.113.9 targeting port 80 (level 10).` and click **Run through Units I–V**. Scroll through the cards. | "Unit I masks the indicators and our bigram model gives its perplexity. Unit II: our HMM with Viterbi against spaCy, the CVE entity, and the CKY table for the toy grammar. Unit III: the triple and our Chu-Liu/Edmonds spanning tree next to spaCy's parse. Unit IV: TF-IDF terms. Unit V: semantic roles." | I–V |
| 9 | 6:30–7:00 | Open **Evaluation**. | "All numbers come from running the code on two full scenarios: ROUGE for the three summaries, NER P/R/F1, triple P/R, held-out perplexity with and without smoothing, silhouette and purity. The gold files are hand-written drafts, and the NER score is inflated because most test sentences follow our own template." | all |

**Backup files if time allows:** `05_normal_noise.json` (shows no High/Critical incidents and "no suspicious IPs");
`synthetic_09_two_attackers.json` (two attackers, two incidents); `synthetic_08_cve_exploit.json` (CVE + MITRE entities and the patch recommendation).
